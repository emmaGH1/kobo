# Reference notes

## ref-touriq.mp4 (TourIQ product film, 90s, 1280×720. Kept locally, not committed)

Copy the **technique**, never the content.

Take:
- **Story arc:** a history hook ("for most of history, a price was a conversation") → the problem → logo reveal → how it thinks → a concrete example (Saturday up, Tuesday down) → the human approves → guardrails → the result → a bookend back to the opening image → end card. Kobo uses the same spine: the notebook opens the film and closes it.
- **Grade:** a dark world with one warm glow behind the UI. Kobo's glow is lavender on graphite instead of orange on brown.
- **Exploded UI:** the real interface on a tilted 3D plane with a spotlight, and single components lifting off with depth of field behind them.
- **Recurring data motif:** the split-flap price (`$ 9 5`) that keeps returning. Kobo's motif is the naira figure written in the book, plus the ₦3,800 / ₦38,000 split.
- **Captions:** one line near the bottom, word by word, one key word in the accent colour.
- **Pace:** something changes every 2–3 seconds; camera moves only when attention shifts.
- **Approve moment:** a cursor clicks "Approve $119". Kobo's version is the trader tapping "1 Save".

Do not take:
- The film and TV clips under the hook. Kobo uses licensed stock or motion only.
- The revenue-growth payoff with bar charts. Kobo has no real usage numbers, so its payoff is the reminder and the trader staying in charge.
- Logos of third-party products.
